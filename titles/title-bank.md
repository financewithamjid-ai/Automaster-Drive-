# Title Bank — Auto Master Drive

Rule: every title is a **search phrase US viewers type**:
`[Model A] [year] vs [Model B] [year]: [number / price / range]`.
Clever / puzzle titles are banned. The winner is never in the title
or the thumbnail.

## Title patterns that work (distilled from 34 US car/EV channels, 26 Oct 2026)

Source: competitor research across carwow, MotorTrend, Edmunds, Out of Spec,
KRIS RIFA, Bjørn Nyland, Fully Charged, Doug DeMuro and 26 more channels.
Only patterns that fit the AMD rule (model + year + "vs" + number) are listed.

### P1. Head-to-head + one decisive number
`[A] vs [B] [year]: [number] vs [number]`
Example: "2026 Toyota bZ vs Tesla Model Y: 11.43 vs 11.81"
Why: the number IS the search query. Our best-performing longs all use it.

### P2. Head-to-head + price gap
`[A] vs [B]: $[price] vs $[price] — The $[gap] Gap`
Example: "Tesla Cybertruck vs Rivian R1T: $92,490 vs $72,990 — The $19,500 Gap"
Why: price is the #1 comparison search. Works best when the gap flips the expected winner.

### P3. Head-to-head + test score
`[A] vs [B]: [score] vs [score] — [Test source] Scores It`
Example: "Kia EV6 GT vs Tesla Model Y Performance: 585 vs 576"
Why: a named test (Edmunds, AMS, CAA) makes the number verifiable, not a claim.

### P4. Head-to-head + range flip (claim vs reality)
`[A] vs [B]: [claim range] vs [tested range] Miles`
Example: "Rivian R2 vs Tesla Model Y: 345 vs 357 Miles — Edmunds Scores It 8.2 to 8.2"
Why: the flip (lab says one thing, road says another) is the hook and the title.

### P5. Fixed-test format (same test, both cars)
`[A] vs [B]: [test name] — [shocking result number]`
Distilled from: Out of Spec 100%-to-0% range test, KRIS RIFA 120 km/h test,
Bjørn Nyland 1000 km challenge.
Why: one repeatable test = a series viewers can compare across videos.
Rule: the "shocking" result must be real — never use SHOCKING when the result is ordinary.

### P6. Old generation vs new generation
`[year] [Model] vs [year] [Model]: How Much Has It Improved?`
Distilled from: Cleanerwatt ("2017 VS 2020 Tesla Model 3").
Why: year-vs-year is a clean search phrase and fits the future-cars niche
(2026 vs 2028 model).

### P7. Price-bracket showdown
`Best [EV truck / SUV] Under $[amount]: [A] vs [B] vs [C]`
Distilled from: Everyday Driver ("8 Under $8,000").
Why: budget searches are high-intent; keep it to models with verified prices.

### P8. Superlative + model — only when the claim is verified
`The MOST [powerful / efficient] [Model]: [number]`
Distilled from: Raiti's Rides ("The MOST powerful Mustang GT").
Why: superlatives pull clicks, but only when the number proves it.
Never use a superlative on an unverified claim.

### P9. Verdict-first question (sparingly)
`[A] vs [B] — [Question the video answers]?`
Distilled from: Redline Reviews ("Is The Kia Telluride The New 3-Row SUV King?"),
Car Confections ("BETTER than the Telluride??").
Why: a question title works when the video genuinely answers it.
Use at most 1 in 4 videos — the number patterns (P1–P4) are the default.

### P10. Claim vs reality (debunk format)
`[Claim]: $[price] [Model] vs Reality — [number] Claims Checked`
Example: "Tesla 2027 Lineup Debunked: $22,000 Model Q vs Reality — 7 Claims Checked"
Why: debunks ride viral claims, but every checked claim must be sourced.
Never present a rumour as a fact to "debunk" it.

## Banned

- Puzzle / clever titles with no model, year or number ("You Won't Believe This EV!").
- The winner announced in the title or thumbnail.
- "SHOCKING" / "INSANE" unless the verified result genuinely shocks.
- A number from one test protocol mixed with a number from another
  (e.g. WLTP range vs EPA range) without labelling each.
